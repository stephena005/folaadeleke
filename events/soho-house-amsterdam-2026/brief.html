02

Purpose and concept

Nigerian Independence Day is usually marked with scale: flags, parades, big rooms. This evening does the opposite. It celebrates the country through one person's story, told slowly, to a hundred people who are close enough to ask a question.

Why this, why now

On 1 October 1960 Nigeria became independent. Sixty-six years on, a generation of Nigerians raised abroad is shaping culture in London, Amsterdam, New York and Lagos at once. Fola Adeleke is one of them: born to Nigerian parents, raised in East London, now leading design at Daily Paper in Amsterdam and making artwork about the family, ceremony and memory that travelled with him.

The evening uses his experience as a lens. What does it mean to be Black, British and Nigerian? How does heritage show up in a fashion campaign, a print, a typeface, a colour choice? What does a Nigerian living room in Hackney have to say to a design studio in Amsterdam? These are the questions the Q&A will work through.

Why Soho House Amsterdam

  • The right scale. A members' club room holds intimacy in a way a gallery or a venue cannot. One hundred people, one conversation, no stage distance.
  • The right audience. Soho House's Amsterdam membership sits across fashion, design, music and film. That is exactly the room Fola's work speaks to, and the one Daily Paper helped build in this city.
  • The right feel. The brief is chic and Afrocentric without costume or cliché. Soho House's interiors, service and standards do most of that work already.

The three ideas the evening must hold

Conversation

A candid, well-moderated Q&A is the centre of the night. Everything else supports it. If the talk lands, the evening lands.

Celebration

Independence is a joyful thing. After the talk the room should loosen: music up, drinks flowing, people meeting. Nigerian, but for everyone in the room.

Craft

Fola is a designer. Details will be noticed: the typeface on the invitation, the lighting on the prints, the order of the canapés. Keep everything considered and restrained.

What success looks like

  • 100 guests in the room, with at least 60 present for the full talk.
  • A conversation people quote afterwards: honest, specific, not a panel of platitudes.
  • Guests staying past 22:00 because the room feels good, not because they were asked to.
  • Photography and a short film that Soho House, Fola and Daily Paper are all happy to publish.
  • A repeatable template: a Soho House "in conversation" format for diaspora creatives, in Amsterdam or another House.

The blurb

Approved copy for the Soho House listing, member app, invitations and social. Please use verbatim or send edits back for sign-off. The long version is for the event page; the short version for the app card, email subject lines and Instagram; the one-liner for the run-of-show and signage.

Long version · c. 200 words · event page

To mark Nigerian Independence Day, Soho House Amsterdam hosts an intimate evening with artist and designer Fola Adeleke.

Nigerian-British, raised in East London and now based between Amsterdam and London, Fola is Lead Designer at Daily Paper and a mixed media artist whose limited-edition prints explore identity, family and cultural memory. His work sits at the meeting point of African heritage and city life, layering texture, portraiture and contemporary visual storytelling.

In a candid Q&A, Fola will talk through the experiences that shaped him: growing up between two cultures, building a career in fashion and design, and how being Black, British and Nigerian informs the way he sees, makes and designs. Expect an honest conversation about heritage, craft and the creative freedom that comes from belonging to more than one place.

The evening is limited to 100 invited guests. Drinks and canapés will be served, with a DJ set moving from Afrobeats and highlife to the wider sounds of the diaspora.

Chic, intimate and unapologetically Afrocentric, this is a celebration of Nigerian independence through the lens of one of its diaspora's most distinctive creative voices.

About Fola Adeleke

Fola Adeleke · portrait supplied by the studio

Biography · for programme and introduction

Fola Adeleke is a Nigerian-British mixed media digital artist and designer raised in East London, whose work explores identity, culture and emotional memory through layered contemporary visual storytelling. Influenced by the intersection of African heritage and urban life, his practice blends texture, symbolism, portraiture and graphic composition to create works that feel both deeply personal and culturally expansive.

Adeleke is the Lead Designer at Daily Paper, where his visual language has been shaped by fashion, architecture, music and contemporary African creativity. Now based between Amsterdam and London, his work carries a strong sense of rhythm, design sensitivity and modern cultural dialogue.

His limited-edition prints are released in small drops through folaadeleke.com, each supplied with a certificate of authenticity.

The work · what guests will see

Kingdom We Carry
Loud Celebration
Recurring themes
  • Family, elders and ceremony
  • Aso-oke, gele and Nigerian dress placed in British and Dutch streets
  • Celebration as resistance: the "Loud" series
  • Portraiture, symbolism, layered texture
  • Monochrome brand, colour reserved for the art

Suggested lines of questioning for the moderator

  • East London, Nigerian household. What did the two worlds look like from inside the front door, and where did they meet?
  • The path into design. Who let you in, and what did you have to unlearn?
  • Daily Paper: an African-diaspora brand built in Amsterdam. What does designing for that mean day to day?
  • When did the art practice start, and why prints rather than canvases?
  • Kingdom We Carry: full Nigerian dress against a London estate. Where does that image come from?
  • Working with AI-assisted tools as a Black artist: control, authorship, and what you refuse to let it decide.
  • What does Nigerian independence mean to someone who has never lived there full-time?
  • What do you want the next Nigerian-British kid in a design studio to have that you didn't?

Format notes

  • Seating. Salon layout: c. 60 seats in loose arcs (banquettes, armchairs, stools) plus standing room at the back and sides. No rows, no stage riser above 20 cm. Fola and the moderator in two armchairs, side-on to each other, not behind a table.
  • Moderator. A journalist, curator or fellow designer who knows the diaspora conversation and can push back warmly. The studio will propose two names; Soho House may also suggest a member TBC.
  • Visuals. Optional single screen or projection showing a slow loop of six to eight works during the talk, otherwise dark. No slides, no logos on screen.
  • Recording. The talk to be filmed on two cameras and audio-captured from the desk for a short edit. Soho House to confirm filming policy for the space and member consent process TBC.

Food and drink

A direction rather than a menu. Soho House's kitchen and bar to propose within it. The principle: Nigerian flavour, Soho House execution. Nothing served in a takeaway box, nothing labelled "fusion".

Drinks

  • Welcome cocktail (one, handed at the door). Suggested: a Chapman, the Lagos club-house classic, rebuilt with quality bitters and a grapefruit twist. Or a hibiscus (zobo) spritz.
  • Bar. House cocktails plus two signatures: a palm-wine or hibiscus-led long drink and a dark spirit serve (Nigerian bar culture leans cognac and whisky). Champagne for the toast.
  • Non-alcoholic. Chilled zobo (hibiscus, ginger, clove) and a ginger-pineapple cooler. Treated with the same care as the cocktails; a meaningful share of the room will not drink.
  • Bar tab. Hosted bar 19:00 – 21:00, then cash bar or continued hosting TBC.

Canapés

Eight to ten passed items over the evening, roughly 8 pieces per guest, weighted towards the reception after the talk. Suggested references for the kitchen:

  • Suya-spiced beef or chicken skewers, yaji dust, red onion
  • Jollof arancini with scotch-bonnet aioli
  • Plantain: crisp kelewele bites, or dodo with a peppered dip
  • Puff-puff, served warm, sugared or with a spiced honey
  • Moin-moin or akara (bean fritters), vegan by default
  • Grilled prawns with pepper sauce
  • Chin-chin as a bar snack in small bowls

Dietary: at least three vegan items and one gluten-free item on rotation. Allergen cards at the bar. Halal meat preferred TBC.

07

Music and sound

Music carries the Afrocentric brief more than anything else in the room. Two sets, one DJ, a clear arc: from listening to dancing.

Set one · 19:00 – 19:55 · arrival

Warm, unhurried, at conversation level. Highlife, Afro-soul, jazz and the softer end of contemporary Afrobeats. Reference points: Ebo Taylor, Tony Allen, Fela's slower side, Asa, Tems, Cleo Sol, Sault, Obongjayar. Guests should notice the music without having to raise their voices.

Set two · 20:55 – 22:45 · reception

Starts on the applause and builds. Afrobeats-led with room for amapiano, highlife classics and diaspora crossover. Reference points: Burna Boy, Wizkid, Asake, Rema, Ayra Starr, Davido, King Sunny Adé, Fela, and the UK-Nigerian sound (Skepta, J Hus, Odumodublvck). Peak around 22:00, then ease down. Nigerian, but built so the whole room can move.

DJ

  • Studio to propose a DJ from Fola's Amsterdam / London network with a Nigerian or diaspora selection; Soho House may counter-propose from its roster TBC.
  • DJ positioned in the room, visible but not central. No booth branding.
  • Fee, travel and rider to be agreed between the studio and DJ; Soho House provides the technical setup.

Sound for the talk

  • Two lapel or headset mics (Fola, moderator) plus two handheld radio mics for audience questions.
  • Speech-tuned PA separate from, or re-EQ'd from, the DJ system. Intelligibility at the back of the room is the test.
  • A sound engineer present for the talk. Desk feed to camera for recording.

Look, feel and artwork

Chic. Intimate. Afrocentric. Creative. Four words, and the fourth is what keeps the first three honest. Nothing themed, nothing hired in from a party supplier. The room should look like Soho House on its best night, with Fola's work as the only colour in it.

Principles

  • Restraint. Fola's brand is monochrome: black, white, one typeface. The interface stays out of the way and the art carries the colour. The room should follow the same rule.
  • The flag, quietly. Green, white, green appears once or twice as a detail (a cocktail, a ribbon on the programme, the floral), never as bunting or a colour wash.
  • Warm and low. Candlelight and lamps over overheads. Prints lit properly; everything else dimmed. Aim for a room people photograph well in without a flash.
  • Textile, not print. If any Nigerian material is used it is real aso-oke or adire as table or lectern cloth, sourced by the studio. No printed "Ankara" patterns on signage.
  • Dress code. On the invitation: "Dress well. Nigerian dress welcomed and encouraged." Expect and celebrate agbada and gele in the room.

Print and signage

  • One A2 or A1 title board at the entrance, black on white, typewritten style, carrying the one-liner and the Fola Adeleke® mark. Studio supplies artwork; Soho House prints or the studio delivers TBC.
  • A folded A5 programme per guest: the blurb, Fola's bio, the works on show, the running order. Studio designs and supplies.
  • Small wall labels for each work: title, year, edition. Studio supplies.
  • No roll-up banners, no step-and-repeat.

Artwork on show

Six to eight limited-edition prints, framed, from the current and recent drops. Proposed selection to include Kingdom We Carry, Loud Celebration, Elders, Family Council, Mother of a Nation and Community; final list confirmed by the studio two weeks out.

  • Display. Hung on existing picture rails or fixings where the space allows; otherwise on black or natural-wood easels supplied by the studio. Grouped in two or three clusters along the guest flow, not lined up in a row.
  • Lighting. Each work individually lit (pin spots or adjustable track). Please confirm what the space has and whether the studio should bring supplementary lamps TBC.
  • Handling. Studio delivers, installs and derigs. Soho House to confirm load-in route, times and insurance position for artwork on site TBC.
  • Sales. No prices displayed and no selling from the floor. Guests who ask are given a card and followed up by the studio. If Soho House prefers a small edition available to members that evening, the studio is open to it TBC.

Photography and film

  • One stills photographer and one videographer (two-camera talk capture plus roaming) commissioned by the studio.
  • Style: reportage, low light, no flash during the talk. Portraits of guests in Nigerian dress against the prints are a priority set.
  • Edited stills within 72 hours; a 60–90 second film within 10 days. Shared usage rights for Soho House, the studio and Daily Paper TBC.
  • Soho House to advise on member photography policy and any no-photo areas.

Commercial model and next steps

Commercial model · for discussion

Option A — Member programming. Soho House hosts the evening as part of its cultural programme. Venue and core F&B covered against a minimum spend or programming budget; the studio covers talent, content and artwork logistics.

  • Simple to run, strongest fit with a members' event.
  • Guest list weighted towards members.

Option B — Private hire with partner. The studio (with Daily Paper or another partner) hires the space. Full control of the guest list and content; Soho House provides venue, F&B and AV at agreed rates.

  • More control, more cost.
  • Opens the door to a brand partner and print edition on the night.

The studio's preference is Option A with a small number of protected non-member invitations. Open to Soho House's view.

Decisions needed from Soho House · by Friday 25 September

  • Date and space confirmed for Thursday 1 October (or alternative) and the commercial model above.
  • Filming and photography policy for the space, and member consent approach.
  • Artwork fixings, lighting and insurance position.
  • RSVP mechanism for non-member guests.
  • Site visit slot in the week of 21 September, with the event manager and, ideally, the sound engineer.

What the studio will deliver next

  • Two moderator proposals and a draft question set, within five working days of date confirmation.
  • DJ proposal and reference playlist.
  • Final artwork list and framed dimensions, two weeks out.
  • Invitation and programme designs for approval, three weeks out.
  • Full run-of-show with names and mobiles, one week out.
